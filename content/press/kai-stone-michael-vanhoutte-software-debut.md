While many startups are built for investors, StoneSystems was built for the people who actually work. The company's all-in-one platform simplifies communication, scheduling, and customer reviews for contractors and service teams — eliminating the need for multiple disconnected apps.

In less than a year, the bootstrapped startup has expanded to a 22-person team and achieved a seven-figure run rate, driven entirely by customer adoption. With its customer-first approach and focus on real-world efficiency, StoneSystems is positioning itself as a practical alternative to the investor-driven software models dominating the industry.

## Designed for Real-World Usability

> "Most software companies try to impress investors," Stone says. "We're trying to impress the guy covered in drywall dust who's still running a business from his truck."

That focus on usability has been the company's advantage. While competitors roll out complex tools that overwhelm small teams, StoneSystems builds features that save time on real jobs. The approach is clear: fewer clicks, fewer headaches, and a smoother workday for people who don't have time to sit through training videos.

## A Partnership That Balances Vision and Precision

Stone and Vanhoutte credit their success to their collaborative approach. Stone brings energy, instinct, and a big-picture mindset. Vanhoutte brings structure, systems, and relentless attention to detail.

> "Kai sees where things are going before most people notice," Vanhoutte says. "My job is to make sure what we build can actually handle the demand when it gets there."

Their contrast has become the company's rhythm: move fast, stay sharp, and always prioritize stability. Every product decision ties back to what customers need most, not what looks good in a pitch deck.

## Building for Results, Not Hype

> "We don't build for hype," Stone says. "We build for results. If it doesn't save time or make someone money, it doesn't belong in the software."

The founders are now preparing to expand the platform's automation and reporting tools while keeping the interface clean and intuitive. The goal: help contractors manage more jobs with less effort, without adding unnecessary complexity.

## Simplifying the Future of Work

For many small businesses, that simplicity is exactly what's been missing in an industry where every minute counts — software should lighten the load, not add to it.

> "Technology should work for people who work," Stone adds. "That's the only kind that lasts."

## About StoneSystems

StoneSystems builds software that simplifies everyday business operations. Designed for contractors and home-service providers, the platform unites communication, scheduling, reviews, and customer management under one roof. With a focus on usability, consistent updates, and dependable performance, StoneSystems helps small teams operate like big ones, without the chaos.
